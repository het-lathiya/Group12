## City buses (AMTS)

The Ahmedabad Municipal Transport Service (AMTS) has run city buses since 1947, and its network reaches most of the city. It is the cheapest way to get around. Older buses are red and white or red and yellow; newer bright red buses have electronic destination boards. Route information is mostly in Gujarati, so it helps to ask people at the stop. Map apps with a public-transit option show nearby AMTS stops and route numbers, but their timings are not always reliable.

You get on at the rear door and get off at the front. A conductor on board sells you a ticket for your destination, so carry small notes. A day pass is sold by the conductor, but for a few short rides paying per ride usually works out better. Buses are often crowded and stop only briefly at most stops (Lal Darwaja, the central terminus, and the end of each route are the exceptions). Ask the conductor or a fellow passenger to warn you before your stop, and move towards the front door early.

AMTS also runs air-conditioned sightseeing buses ("Dekho Amdavad") from the Law Garden Civic Centre, with morning and afternoon city tours and a Sunday tour of temples.

## Bus rapid transit (BRTS, Janmarg)

Janmarg, the bus rapid transit system run by Ahmedabad Janmarg Limited since 2009, uses silver buses on dedicated lanes in the middle of the road, so it is usually faster than ordinary traffic. Its coverage is limited, so not every area can be reached. Stations are signed in English and Hindi.

Buy your ticket at the station before boarding and keep it until you leave, because it is checked at the exit. Buses have automatic doors, and on-board announcements and displays show the next stations. BRTS and AMTS tickets are separate systems, but you can combine the two with auto-rickshaws for a longer trip.

## Auto-rickshaws

Yellow-and-green auto-rickshaws are everywhere. They are always cheaper than a taxi, though for longer trips they cost more than the bus. They are small and not very comfortable, and some drivers drive roughly; you can ask the driver to slow down. Wave one down by calling "auto" or "rickshaw". Most drivers understand Hindi and some English.

Agree the fare before you get in, and remember that night fares differ from day fares. Some drivers overcharge visitors, for example by taking a longer route, so it helps to know roughly how far you are going.

## Taxis

Taxis are rarely found on the street, because auto-rickshaws dominate. Book one in advance, through your hotel (often the more expensive option), online, or at the government-authorised taxi stand outside the main railway station, and agree the fare with the driver.

## Arriving by air, rail and road

Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel International Airport is at Hansol, north-east of the centre near the river, between Shahibaug and Naroda. From the airport, use the prepaid booth for an auto-rickshaw or taxi, or arrange a pick-up with your hotel; AMTS buses also serve the airport.

The main railway station, Ahmedabad Junction, is in Kalupur, with services to Mumbai, Delhi, Jaipur, Vadodara and many other cities. Maninagar and Sabarmati Junction are other stations, and not every train stops at each one. All three are served by BRTS and AMTS.

State buses of the Gujarat State Road Transport Corporation (GSRTC) arrive from across Gujarat at the central Geeta Mandir terminal, which connects to BRTS and AMTS at Astodia Darwaja. Buses get crowded, so booking ahead is wise. By road, the Vadodara–Ahmedabad Expressway reaches the south of the city, and an inter-city highway links Ahmedabad with Gandhinagar.
