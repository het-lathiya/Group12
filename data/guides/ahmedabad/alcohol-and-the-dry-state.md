## Prohibition since 1960

Gujarat has been a dry state since 1 May 1960, the day the old Bombay State was split into Gujarat and Maharashtra. The Bombay Prohibition Act of 1949 is still in force there, and the state's law bans making, storing, selling and drinking alcoholic drinks. Bombay State itself had prohibition for some years before the split.

## Permits for visitors

The law makes an exception for visitors:

- Foreign nationals and visitors from other Indian states can apply online for a liquor permit.
- A permit holder can buy alcohol only at licensed shops, of which there are about 35 across the state, nine of them in Ahmedabad.
- At the shop you must show a physical copy of the permit.
- When the permit expires, any alcohol you have not finished must be handed over to the district collector.

## GIFT City

Since December 2023, controlled drinking has been allowed inside GIFT City (Gujarat International Finance Tec-City) near Gandhinagar. The exception covers only its permanent employees and authorised visitors.

## Illegal liquor

Despite the ban, smuggling and illicit sales are common, and locals use the slang word "folder" for a bootlegger who delivers alcohol on request. Illegal liquor can be dangerous: there have been deaths from methanol-contaminated drink, including in 2022. Gujarat is the only Indian state whose law allows the death penalty for making or selling home-made liquor that causes deaths, a provision added in 2009.

Five petitions challenging the ban have been filed before the Gujarat High Court; most argue that it violates the right to privacy and seek permission to drink in private.
