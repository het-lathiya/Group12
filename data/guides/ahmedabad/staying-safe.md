## A relatively safe city

Ahmedabad is considered a relatively safe city. People are generally friendly, and the police help in emergencies. Because alcohol is prohibited in Gujarat, late-night drunken rowdiness is less common than in many other large cities. The city police run about 35 main police stations as well as smaller posts.

## Emergency numbers

- 108: general emergencies and ambulance
- 100: police
- 101: fire

## Getting around without being overcharged

Overcharging is a common problem for visitors:

- Some auto-rickshaw drivers try to cheat people who look like outsiders, for example by taking a longer route.
- Agree the fare before you get in, and remember that day and night fares are different.
- Knowing roughly how far you are going makes it harder to be taken the long way round.
- If a driver drives too fast or roughly, ask them to slow down.

City buses are often very crowded, so move towards the exit well before your stop.

## Shopping

Haggling is expected at roadside stalls and markets. If you would rather not bargain, use a fixed-price shop, although prices there can be higher.

## Food and drink

If you are new to India, start with well-known and well-run restaurants. Be careful with drinks from small stalls; larger, reputable places are a safer choice for lassi and fruit juice.

Illegal alcohol from bootleggers is widely available but risky. Stay within the official permit system described in the alcohol guide.

## Phones

Foreign nationals buying a prepaid SIM card need:

- a photocopy of the passport and visa pages;
- a passport-size photo;
- proof of an address in India.

The country code is +91, and Ahmedabad's area code is 79.
