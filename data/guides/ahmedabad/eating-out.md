## What to expect

Ahmedabad has restaurants almost everywhere. Most local food is vegetarian, and Gujarati cooking tends to be on the sweet side. Non-vegetarian food has become easier to find as national and international chains have opened. The city is also known for its ice cream, thanks to the state's large dairy output and a local love of sweets.

## Dishes to try

Signature Gujarati dishes include:

- **dhokla**, a steamed savoury cake;
- **khandvi**, soft rolls of gram flour;
- **handvo**, a savoury cake;
- **bhajiya**, fritters;
- **shrikhand**, a sweet strained yogurt.

## Thali dining halls

Gujarati dining halls serve unlimited thali meals: many small dishes, served in several courses. A typical spread includes:

- salads and farsan snacks;
- rotis and puris;
- dals and vegetable curries;
- rice;
- sweets such as gulab jamun or a mango dessert;
- buttermilk.

## Street food

In the evening and late into the night, two areas are especially popular for street food:

- the Khau Galli (food lane) near Law Garden;
- the food market at Manek Chowk in the old city.

Typical snacks include pani puri, chaat, sandwiches and Indo-Chinese dishes. In the west of the city, S.G. Road has become a long strip of restaurants. The monsoon season brings corn snacks.

## Drinks

There are no bars, and restaurants generally do not serve alcohol; see the guide on alcohol rules. Lassi in many flavours and fresh fruit juice are sold all over the city. At small stalls, take care with hygiene, and prefer larger, well-kept places.

## Tips for visitors

- If you are new to Indian food, start with well-known, well-run restaurants.
- Locals, especially younger people, are a good source of recommendations.
- Traditional Gujarati sweets from local sweet shops are a popular buy.
