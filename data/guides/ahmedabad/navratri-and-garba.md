## When it happens

Navratri ("nine nights") is one of Gujarat's main festivals. The autumn festival, Sharada Navaratri, falls in the Hindu month of Ashvin (September to October), and Dussehra marks its end.

## The garbo and the Garba dance

Prayers during Navratri centre on the garbo, a clay pot with a lamp inside. Traditional pots have 27 holes, linked to the 27 nakshatras (lunar mansions), and the light shining through them is said to represent many souls coming from one source.

Garba is the group folk dance of the festival:

- Dancers move in circles, to live music or devotional songs.
- A circle can grow to hundreds or even thousands of people.
- Afterwards, dancers and spectators stay on to socialise and eat together.

## Dandiya raas

In dandiya raas, partners face each other and strike decorated sticks, the dandiyas, in rhythm, usually to an eight-beat pattern. The tempo builds as the dance goes on.

## What people wear

- **Women** usually wear a chaniya choli: a long flared skirt with a fitted blouse and a matching scarf, often decorated with mirror-work and embroidery.
- **Men** traditionally wore a kediyu, a waist-length garment pleated at the chest, with loose trousers and a turban. Today many wear a kurta with churidar trousers.

## Fasting

Many people fast during Navratri. Some fast completely for one day; others fast partly across all nine days, avoiding grains or taking only liquids.

## For visitors

Temples and shrines of folk goddesses are traditional places for Navratri fairs and garba dancing.
