## A World Heritage City

In July 2017 the historic walled city of Ahmedabad, often called Old Ahmedabad, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The protected core covers roughly 536 hectares, with a buffer zone around it. Its traditional housing clusters, the pols, had already helped place the city on UNESCO's Tentative List.

## East and west of the river

The Sabarmati river splits Ahmedabad into two very different halves. The old city lies on the east bank. It is dense and crowded, with packed bazaars, tightly clustered housing and a great many places of worship, and it also holds the main railway station, the main post office and buildings from the Sultanate and British periods. Bhadra, around Bhadra Fort, is its historic centre, with Victoria Garden at the southern edge of the fort area.

The western side grew after the river was bridged: Ellis Bridge was built in 1875 and later joined by Nehru Bridge. The west is where you find educational institutions, offices, housing, malls and cinemas, along Ashram Road, C.G. Road and the Sarkhej–Gandhinagar Highway.

## Walls and gates

The walled city was fortified in 1487 with an outer wall about ten kilometres around, twelve gates and nearly two hundred bastions.

## Pols

A pol is a housing cluster in which families of one community, linked by caste, profession or religion, lived together. Mahurat Pol is said to be the first pol in the city. Some areas of the old city are known for a craft: tie-dyed bandhani fabric from Rangeela Pol, traditional mojdi footwear from Madhupura, and Ganesha idols made in Gulbai Tekra. Pols are homes, so when you walk through one, remember that you are in a residential neighbourhood.

## Manek Chowk

Manek Chowk, the old city's best-known square, changes through the day. It sells vegetables in the morning, becomes a jewellery market later in the day and turns into a busy street-food market in the evening. Older areas nearby, such as Bhatiyar Gali, Kalupur and Jamalpur, are where to find Mughlai non-vegetarian food in a mostly vegetarian city.

## Riverfront and lakes

The Sabarmati Riverfront opened in 2012, and water brought from the Narmada keeps the river flowing all year. The city has three lakes within its limits: Kankaria, built in 1451 in Maninagar; the small leisure lake at Vastrapur; and the large Chandola lake, popular with birds and evening walkers. Apart from the low Thaltej–Jodhpur Tekra hills, the city is almost flat.
