## About these notes

These notes are a team-written summary of what the pages listed at the end say about step-free access and getting around with limited mobility in Ahmedabad. Accessibility varies a great deal from place to place, so check each place's own information before you go.

## Public transport

- **BRTS (Janmarg).** BRTS stations are wheelchair accessible. The exceptions are the RTO Circle and Kalupur Railway Station stops, which are simple roadside shelters. Tickets are sold at the stations. The buses have automatic doors, and on-board announcements and displays show the next stations, which helps visitors who cannot easily read stop signs.
- **Metro.** Thaltej metro station is listed as accessible on its Wikipedia page.
- **AMTS city buses.** These buses are often crowded and stop only briefly at most stops. You board at the rear door and leave by the front, which can be hard for anyone who moves slowly.
- **Auto-rickshaws.** They are small and not very spacious, and some drivers drive roughly. You can ask the driver to slow down.

## Railway station

At Ahmedabad's main railway station in Kalupur, most platforms have a lift, an escalator or both. A 2025 news report said that Platform 12, the one linked to the metro station, had neither, which caused difficulty for older passengers and people with limited mobility. Railway officials said the station's redevelopment, due for completion in 2027, will add lifts, escalators and foot overbridges.

## The old city

The old walled city is dense, with packed bazaars and closely clustered housing in its pols. The city's Heritage Walk through this area is done on foot. Some monuments are raised: the courtyard of Rani no Hajiro, the queens' tombs near Manek Chowk, sits on a platform about ten feet above the street.

## Akshardham, Gandhinagar

Swaminarayan Akshardham lends wheelchairs free of charge, after the security check, to people with disabilities, elderly visitors and anyone else who needs one. There are restrooms throughout the campus.

## Heat

Summer in Ahmedabad is very hot. The CDC's advice for India is to eat and drink regularly, wear light, loose clothing and limit physical activity when temperatures are high.
