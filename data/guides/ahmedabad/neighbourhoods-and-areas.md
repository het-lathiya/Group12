## Two banks of the Sabarmati

The original city was built on open land on the east bank of the Sabarmati river. Later growth spread to both banks, and the city now stretches far to the west. The old town on the east bank is where the cheaper hotels tend to be, while the west holds most of the newer business and residential districts.

The Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation divides the city into zones. The zone names can confuse visitors, because they do not always match geography: Motera, for example, lies in the north of the city but belongs to the West Zone.

## Central Zone: the walled city

The Central Zone is mostly the old walled city, with its Sultanate and Mughal-era buildings. The walled city once had twelve gates and was centred on Bhadra Fort. The main railway station is here, in Kalupur, and the zone includes the historic areas of Khadia, Dariapur and Raikhad.

## North and East Zones

The North Zone includes Naroda, an industrial and developing area, Asarva, where the Civil Hospital is, and Sardarnagar. The international airport is at Hansol in this part of the city. The East Zone is mostly industrial and includes Nikol, Bapunagar, Odhav and Vastral.

## South Zone

The South Zone includes Maninagar, Narol, Vatva and Kankaria, home to Kankaria Lake. The expressway to Vadodara begins in this zone.

## West Zone

The West Zone includes Navrangpura, Usmanpura, Paldi, Sabarmati, Motera, Chandkheda and Vasna. Ashram Road and C.G. Road are its main business streets. Motera and Chandkheda are suburbs in the north, and Vasna in the south is partly industrial.

## New West Zone

The New West Zone is made up of newer, still-growing suburbs: Vastrapur, home to the Indian Institute of Management and Vastrapur Lake, Bodakdev, Thaltej, Sarkhej, Gota and Juhapura. Bodakdev and Vastrapur are commercial centres, and S.G. Road (the Sarkhej–Gandhinagar Highway) has become a long strip of restaurants.

## Where to shop by area

The old city's markets are at Teen Darwaja, Dhalgarwad, Ratan Pole and Manek Chowk, with Lal Darwaja nearby. Newer shopping is found along C.G. Road and the S.G. Highway near Vastrapur and Gurukul. The street stalls around Law Garden are a good place to look for handicrafts and traditional Gujarati clothes.
